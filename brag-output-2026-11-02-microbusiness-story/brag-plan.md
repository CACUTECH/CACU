Storyboard and narration:

1. evening: The shop is closed. The books are not.
Narration: It is eight in the evening. The shop is closed, but Bisi is still adding up the day. She has worked hard. The question is simple: what did the business actually earn?
Source/boundary: Bisi is a fictional composite. No customer testimonial is implied.

2. manual: One business. Too many loose ends.
Narration: A sale sits in a notebook. A supplier bill is folded beside the till. A customer payment is remembered, but not recorded. When records are scattered, even an experienced owner can struggle to see the whole picture.
Source/boundary: Illustrative story informed by published research; not a filmed real business.

3. evidence: Manual records are a documented challenge.
Narration: This challenge is documented. In a 2020 study of one hundred and twenty business owners in Abeokuta South, fifty nine point two percent kept financial records manually themselves, without professional help. This was a local study, not a national estimate.
Source/boundary: Olajide & Obialo (2020), JEMT 26(5), 34?43. Local purposive sample; n = 120.

4. brand: Grow With Ease
Narration: CACU brings the daily business record into one workspace. For Nigerian businesses selling products, services, or both, it connects supported operations with accounting and management reporting. Let us follow Bisi through a more organised working day.
Source/boundary: Product ? Service ? Hybrid. Feature availability depends on setup.

5. sale: Record the sale while it is fresh.
Narration: A customer buys two tins of milk. In point of sale, Bisi finds the item, checks the cart, chooses the payment method, and records the sale. The receipt gives her a record she can return to later.
Source/boundary: Illustrative data. Transfer recording does not verify bank settlement.

6. stock: Know what moved. Know what remains.
Narration: Stock is more than a number on a shelf. CACU keeps item records, stock changes and adjustment history. Supported valuation controls help track inventory cost and reviewed write downs, so changes have an explanation rather than disappearing into a notebook.
Source/boundary: Configured valuation policy required. Moving weighted average; not a FIFO engine.

7. invoice: An invoice. A payment. A trail.
Narration: For a customer who buys on account, Bisi records the invoice and later records the payment. Customer records, invoices, receipts and supported credit documents help her follow the transaction, instead of relying on memory or a crossed out name.
Source/boundary: Supported completed-obligation workflows; no automatic debt collection.

8. supplier: Supplier bills should not be surprises.
Narration: The supplier side matters too. Record the bill, keep the supplier details, and record payments against it. Unpaid and part paid bills stay visible in the workflow. CACU records the payment; it does not send money from a bank account.
Source/boundary: Native accounting and reviewed supplier mappings required.

9. expenses: Small costs still count.
Narration: Transport, supplies and other business costs can be easy to miss at the end of a busy day. Recording supported expenses alongside income gives Bisi and her accountant a more complete basis for understanding what the business has earned.
Source/boundary: Sample categories and amounts. Accurate records still depend on accurate input.

10. ledger: The activity connects to the books.
Narration: After the accounting setup and opening position are reviewed, supported transactions flow into balanced double entry records. The chart of accounts, journals, general ledger and trial balance give the accountant a traceable structure beneath the owner friendly screens.
Source/boundary: Reviewed chart, business opening approval and accounting activation required.

11. reports: From records to clearer decisions.
Narration: Now Bisi can review the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement in one reports area. PDF and Excel exports help her discuss the figures with her accountant. These are management reports, not a promise of finance or an audit opinion.
Source/boundary: Illustrative management statements. No loan approval or IFRS certification claim.

12. cash: Sales are not profit. Profit is not cash.
Narration: Imagine sales of one hundred and twenty thousand naira, stock costs of eighty thousand, and other expenses of twenty thousand. That leaves twenty thousand before other charges in this simplified example. A separate cash view matters because customer and supplier payment timing can differ.
Source/boundary: Illustrative arithmetic only; simplified period example, not actual CACU results.

13. people: Your people are part of the picture.
Narration: As the business grows, CACU also helps organise employee information and attendance. Owners and admins can prepare and review monthly payroll drafts. The boundary is clear: this workflow does not pay employees or post payroll into the accounting ledger.
Source/boundary: Owner/Admin access. Payroll drafts are not payouts or accounting postings.

14. controls: Give access with accountability.
Narration: An assistant can help run the shop without everyone sharing the same authority. Business roles control access, audit history helps explain changes, and configured secure uploads keep supporting files within the reviewed workflow. The owner still has responsibility for reviewing the records.
Source/boundary: Secure uploads require configured service and storage. Email delivery is not included.

15. ai: Ask better questions of your business.
Narration: With the server side provider configured, the CACU AI assistant can help answer questions using business and financial context. Start with a practical question about the records. Treat its response as assistance to review, not an accountant signing off the books.
Source/boundary: AI setup required. No fabricated generated answer or professional approval is shown.

16. setup: A supported start. A routine you can keep.
Narration: Begin with the correct business mode, clean item and party records, and reviewed opening balances and account mappings. Keep the internet connection active while saving. Advanced scheduling, storefront publishing, offline sync and online subscription billing are not available in this release.
Source/boundary: Owner/Admin approval and accountant involvement remain part of activation.

17. support: Software helps. Good habits make it stick.
Narration: Research also points to the human side. An International Growth Centre pilot in Nigeria reported more consistent profit recall with digital records and highlighted personalised support for adoption. The lesson for Bisi is practical: start small, record consistently, and review the numbers with support.
Source/boundary: IGC / Abiola Oyebanjo, policy brief published 5 March 2025. Not a CACU evaluation.
