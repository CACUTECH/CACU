# Batches and expiry dates (Doc 18 R3 "E09 counts and batches", part 2)

**Owner decision of 18 November 2026:**
- Products marked "track batches" get a batch number and expiry date when goods are received.
- CACU keeps how many of each batch are left, and takes stock leaving from the batch that expires first.
- It lists what expires within 30 days or has expired.
- Only those products change how stock leaves.

## What happens

**Stock › Batches & expiry** (`/inventory/batches`):
- **Turning on "Track batches"** is for Owners and Admins. The stock on hand at that moment is shown as "no batch recorded". Turning it off forgets the batches and leaves the stock total as it is.
- **Each tracked product** lists its batches: number, quantity left, expiry date and whether it's in date.
- **"Expired or expiring within 30 days"** lists batches still in stock that need attention. Each has **Write off**, which records a stock adjustment taking stock from that batch. The usual approval limit applies, and writing off at a loss goes to Inventory adjustments and write-offs.

**Receiving goods:**
- For a product that tracks batches, the receiving form asks for the batch number and expiry date. The expiry date is required.
- Goods already past their expiry date are refused.
- A batch number or expiry date on a product that doesn't track batches is refused, so the information isn't silently lost.

**Stock leaving,** whatever the reason (sale, invoice, job, adjustment, count, return to a supplier), is taken in this order. A transfer between outlets doesn't change the business's total, so it doesn't change batches.
1. The batch the action names, if it names one: writing off a batch, or returning a receipt's goods to the supplier.
2. Batches still in date, earliest expiry first.
3. Stock with no batch or expiry recorded.
4. Expired batches, last. Expired stock is written off rather than sold, so it shouldn't be taken by a sale first.

**Returning goods to a supplier** takes back the batch that receipt brought in, not the one expiring first.

**Stock coming back without a batch,** such as a refund or a count that found more, goes into "no batch recorded".

## How it is enforced

Batches follow the product's stock total in the database, the same way per-outlet stock does: an `AFTER UPDATE OF stock_quantity` trigger on `public.catalog_items` (`accounting.batch_follow`). So every route that changes stock keeps batches in step, including the legacy functions.

- **Naming a batch:** the accounting API names the batch for an action with transaction settings. `cacu.batch_no` and `cacu.batch_expiry` are set for stock coming in, by a goods receipt line. `cacu.batch_id` is set for stock going out, by a write-off or a supplier return. The settings are cleared after each line.
- **Tables:**
  - `accounting.batch_item`: which products track batches.
  - `accounting.stock_batch`: each batch's number, expiry and quantity. A batch is one number and expiry date per product.
  - `accounting.goods_receipt_line.batch_id` records the batch a receipt created.
  - `accounting.stock_adjustment.batch_id` records the batch an adjustment takes from. It's set when the adjustment is requested, since adjustments can't be edited afterwards.
- **If stock leaves beyond what the batches hold,** the shortfall shows against "no batch recorded" until counted.

**Limits:**
- Batches are kept for the whole business, not per outlet.
- The batch taken by a sale isn't printed on the receipt.
- An offline sale takes its batch when it syncs.
